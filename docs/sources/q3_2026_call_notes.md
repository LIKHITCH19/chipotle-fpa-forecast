# Notes from Chipotle's Q3 2026 earnings call (Oct 28, 2026)

Quotes I used for the variance write-up and the reforecast. Transcript:
https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/29/chipotle-cmg-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/

## What happened in Q3
- Menu price: "the impact in Q3 was around 2.7%" (Adam Rymer, CFO). Average check was only +1.3%, so mix took off about 1.4 points.
- Other operating costs (16.0% of sales): increase "primarily driven by higher marketing, delivery mix and inflation across several items, including utilities and maintenance."
- Labor (25.7%): "driven by sales volumes, wage inflation and higher labor usage in support of promotional activity", plus discrete insurance costs.

## Q4 2026 outlook
- Comps: "we anticipate that our Q4 comps will actually be around a plus 2%"; October trends "have softened, consistent with the broader industry".
- Menu price: "about 3% in Q4, bringing total pricing in 2026 to around 2%".
- Cost of sales "around 30.5%", with underlying cost of sales inflation "around 3%".
- Labor "around 26%"; other operating costs "around 16%", marketing "in the high 3% range".
- Restaurant-level margin "just under 22%"; full year in the "mid-23% range".
- G&A "around $189 million on a non-GAAP basis".
- Tax: underlying 24% to 26% for 2026.

## 2027
- Openings: 340 to 360 company-owned restaurants in the U.S. and Canada, plus 15 to 25 outside.
- Inflation "in the low to mid-single-digit range"; pricing back near 2% is "just not looking like that's going to happen in 2027 at this point".
- Marketing: "3% is the right range for us to think about".
- Comps: "Too early to give any kind of guide".
- Margin: "call it a mid-24% margin is kind of the baseline" for 2027. This assumes pricing offsets cost inflation
  and transactions grow at least a point to cover new-restaurant dilution; traffic above that falls through at
  about 40%.
